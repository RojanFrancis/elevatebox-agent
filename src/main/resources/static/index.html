How it works

Four systems, one conversation

The customer only sees a WhatsApp chat and a phone call. Behind it, a Spring Boot service coordinates WhatsApp, a voice AI platform and Gemini, and every step is checked, rate-limited and recorded.

They ask on WhatsApp

Meta's webhook delivers the message. The signature is verified, repeats are ignored, and “call” in the text counts as consent.

Meta Cloud APIHMAC-SHA256

The agent calls them

A cooldown and a daily cap are checked, then Bolna dials from an Indian number with the customer's name passed to the agent.

BolnaVapicooldown

Details arrive mid-call

When the customer asks for pricing or examples, the AI calls a tool on the server, which sends a WhatsApp once per call.

Every lead is rated

After the call, Gemini reads the transcript and returns a rating, summary, follow-up and any callback time. The lead is saved.

Engineering

Built like it handles real customers

Phone calls cost money and webhooks get retried, so the service is careful about who it calls, how often, and what happens when something fails.

Consent first

Nobody is cold-called. A call only happens after the customer asks, at most once per number per day, with a global daily cap.

Verified webhooks

Meta's messages are checked with HMAC-SHA256. Voice-platform callbacks and tools need a separate secret, compared in constant time.

Safe to retry

Repeated deliveries are de-duplicated, so a call is analysed once and the mid-call WhatsApp goes out once, however often a webhook fires.

Answers instantly

Webhooks reply at once and the AI work runs on a bounded thread pool. A full queue answers 503 so the sender retries.

Fails gracefully

A missed call frees the cooldown and invites the customer to ask again. Gemini errors fall back to backup models and never crash a call.

143 tests in CI

Every push runs the test suite on GitHub Actions: signatures, limits, de-duplication, tool calls and the AI parsing.
